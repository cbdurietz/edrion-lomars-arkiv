# Aeteras system

The **Aeteras system** is a binary star system consisting of two stellar components: **Aeteras A** (also known as *Aeterion*), a G2V yellow dwarf, and **Aeteras B** (also known as *Umbrix*), an M5 III red giant. The system hosts at least eight known planets, all orbiting the primary star, as well as one confirmed natural satellite, a trojan companion world, and a periodically visiting long-period comet-like body known as *Vandraren* (the Wanderer). The inhabited world **Prima** (Aeteras A III) orbits the primary star at 1.1 AU and is the only confirmed site of complex life in the system.

## Stellar components

### Aeteras A (Aeterion)

Aeteras A, commonly referred to as *Aeterion* or simply *the sun* in most cultural traditions on Prima, is a G2V yellow dwarf and the gravitational and luminous centre of the system. It is a near-perfect solar analogue with a mass of 1.0 solar masses, a radius of 1.0 solar radii, and a luminosity of 1.0 solar luminosities. Its surface temperature of approximately 5,780 K produces warm white-gold light. In certain religious traditions on Prima, the star is referred to as *Aetheria*.

|---|---|

| Mass | 1.0 M☉ |
| Radius | 1.0 R☉ |
| Luminosity | 1.0 L☉ |
| Surface temperature | 5,780 K |
| Colour | Warm white-gold |

### Aeteras B (Umbrix)

Aeteras B, known in worldbuilding tradition as *Umbrix*, is an M5 III red giant and the secondary component of the binary system. With a mass of approximately 0.4 solar masses, it is a low-mass star in an advanced evolutionary stage — a configuration that implies an extraordinarily old system, as stars of this mass require immense timescales to evolve off the main sequence. Its expanded envelope gives it a radius of approximately 100 solar radii and a luminosity of roughly 10 solar luminosities, despite its relatively cool surface temperature of around 3,200 K.

Umbrix orbits the system barycentre at a semi-major axis of 30 AU from Aeteras A, with a low eccentricity of 0.08 and an orbital inclination of approximately 6° relative to the primary planet plane. Its orbital period is approximately 139 years. From the surface of Prima, Umbrix presents an apparent angular diameter of roughly 1.8°, making it approximately 3.5 times the apparent diameter of Earth's full moon. It appears as a large, deep crimson disk in the sky, and its slow positional drift across the stellar background — measurable over a human lifetime — has made it a foundational object in the calendrical, mythological, and astrological traditions of Prima's civilisations.

The system barycentre lies approximately 8.6 AU from Aeteras A, placing it beyond the gas giant Keveron (3.2 AU) but interior to the ice giant Nycthor (8.1 AU).

#### Habitable zone and planetary potential

The evolved luminosity of Umbrix places its circumstellar habitable zone at approximately 1–3 AU from the star — a substantial outward shift compared to what would be expected for a main-sequence star of similar mass. Planets within this zone would not be subject to significant tidal locking forces, as the tidal locking timescale scales approximately with the sixth power of orbital distance; at 1–3 AU, rotation periods would be preserved on timescales far exceeding the current age of the system. Any habitable worlds in this zone would therefore likely maintain diurnal cycles and axial seasons.

The light environment of the Umbrix habitable zone would differ markedly from that of Prima. The strongly red-shifted spectrum of an M5 giant would favour photosynthetic pigments adapted to near-infrared wavelengths, and ultraviolet flux would be negligible. Because Umbrix is in a late evolutionary stage, its luminosity is slowly increasing as the stellar envelope continues to expand, meaning the habitable zone is gradually migrating outward. Any biospheres in the Umbrix system would exist under conditions of long-term stellar brightening.

Umbrix's Hill sphere — the region within which it can gravitationally retain orbiting bodies against the pull of Aeteras A — extends to approximately 12 AU. Stable long-term orbits are generally possible within roughly one third of that, or about 4 AU. No planets of Umbrix have been confirmed or observed from Prima.

> **INFO:** *Umbrix* appears in minor tabletop products and on World Anvil as a character name, and is phonetically close to *Umbriel* (a real moon of Uranus, also used in Elder Scrolls lore). Not a direct conflict, but the name exists in the broader fantasy space. Monitor.

## Planetary system

All eight known planets orbit Aeteras A in prograde, roughly coplanar orbits with inclinations within 2° of each other. Orbital periods are calculated from Kepler's third law based on the given semi-major axes and stellar mass.

### Aeteras A I — Thraxis

*Thraxis* (*Den brände brodern*, "the burned brother") is the innermost planet of the system, a small, airless rock world comparable to Mercury. Its proximity to Aeterion and likely tidal locking have rendered its surface barren and heavily irradiated. It is rarely visible to the naked eye from Prima due to its closeness to the sun.

### Aeteras A II — Varun

*Varun* (*Den beslöjade ugnen*, "the veiled furnace"; also *Den dolda lågan*, "the hidden flame") is the second planet, a Venus-like world with a dense greenhouse atmosphere. Its thick cloud cover conceals a superheated surface. From Prima, Varun is visible as a brilliant morning or evening star — the brightest wandering object in Prima's sky after Sekara.

### Aeteras A III — Prima

*Prima* ("the living world") is the third planet and the only confirmed inhabited world in the Aeteras system. It hosts the continents of Ardenar, Kyrathis, Serthara, and others, and supports a temperate climate with seas, landmasses, and pronounced seasons. At 1.1 AU from a 1.0 L☉ star, Prima receives approximately 83% of the solar irradiance Earth receives from the Sun; its climate is maintained at habitable temperatures through an appropriate atmospheric composition.

### Aeteras A IV — Sekara

*Sekara* (*Den döda systern*, "the dead sister"; also *Tvillingsjälen*, "the twin soul") occupies the same orbital path as Prima in a stable 1:1 resonance at the L5 Lagrange point, trailing Prima by 60°. This configuration — two planet-mass bodies sharing an orbit — is dynamically stable and results in Sekara maintaining a near-constant angular separation of approximately 60° from Aeterion as seen from Prima. Sekara is therefore a permanent evening star, never visible near midnight, and never rising far above the horizon before setting a few hours after the sun. It is the brightest object in Prima's night sky.

Sekara is described as having a dark, reddish surface with unexplained luminous phenomena, and it has been a central object in mythological and astrological systems across Prima's cultures.

### Aeteras A V — Thalorn

*Thalorn* (*Den blå systern*, "the blue sister") is a super-Earth located just beyond the inner system's habitable zone. At 1.7 AU from Aeterion, it receives approximately 35% of Earth's solar irradiance, making it cold but not necessarily lifeless. Its substantial mass allows retention of a thick atmosphere capable of supporting significant greenhouse warming, producing heavy cloud cover that gives the planet a striking blue-marbled appearance from space. The possibility of liquid water in volcanic or subsurface environments has not been ruled out.

### Aeteras A VI — Keveron

*Keveron* (*Den store väktaren*, "the great guardian") is the largest planet in the system and a Jupiter-like gas giant. Its position near the system's frost line — the boundary beyond which volatile compounds condense into solid grains, generally around 3.0 AU for a solar analogue — is consistent with standard models of giant planet formation. Keveron plays a significant gravitational role in the system, deflecting long-period cometary bodies and reducing the rate of inner system impacts. From Prima, it is visible as a bright, slowly moving point of light.

### Aeteras A VIII — Nycthor

*Nycthor* (*Den nattlige*, "the nocturnal one") is the outermost known planet of the system and a smaller ice giant. At 8.1 AU it lies within the critical stability radius of approximately 9.6 AU imposed by the gravitational influence of Umbrix — giving it a margin of approximately 1.5 AU. As a result, Nycthor is the most gravitationally perturbed planet in the system, and its orbital parameters are expected to show measurable secular variation over century-long timescales. Observers on Prima with sufficient long-term astronomical records would be able to detect these deviations — a potential avenue for precisely characterising Umbrix's mass and orbit independently of direct observation.

## Natural satellites

### Vael — moon of Prima

*Vael* is Prima's only known natural satellite and the dominant timekeeping body in Prima's civilisations. Its 30-day orbital period forms the basis of the *vael* calendar unit, dividing the 421-day year into 14 vaelar plus one excess day. Vael is somewhat larger than Earth's Moon and exerts a tidal force approximately 15% stronger. Its apparent angular diameter of roughly 0.53° is slightly larger than the Moon as seen from Earth.

## Other bodies

### Vandraren (the Wanderer)

*Vandraren* is a large icy body in an extremely elliptical, near-polar orbit that carries it from a perihelion of 1.5–2 AU — crossing the inner planetary system — to an aphelion of approximately 60–80 AU, well beyond the orbit of Umbrix. Its orbital inclination of roughly 90° means it approaches the planetary plane almost perpendicularly, producing a steep, dramatic arc across Prima's sky during its infrequent inner-system passages.

Vandraren's orbit is gravitationally influenced by both Aeteras A and Umbrix, and exhibits long-term secular variations as a result. It passes through the inner system approximately once every 100–120 years — roughly every six to eight human generations — appearing as a brilliant comet with a prominent tail crossing the sky at an unexpected angle. This periodicity, too long to be within living memory but short enough to be recorded in texts, has given Vandraren an outsized role in apocalyptic traditions, omen lore, and generational mythology across Prima's cultures.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Perihelion | 1.5–2 AU |
| Aphelion | ~60–80 AU |
| Orbital period | ~100–120 years |
| Eccentricity | ~0.9 |
| Inclination | ~90° (polar) |
| Diameter | 30–80 km |

> **INFO:** *Vandraren* is the Swedish word for "the wanderer" — an original descriptive name in the campaign's native language. No fictional work conflicts possible. Cleared.

## Observation from Prima

### Aeterion

Aeterion dominates the daytime sky of Prima as a warm, white-gold disk. Its apparent size and colour are comparable to the Sun as seen from Earth.

### Umbrix

Umbrix is visible from Prima as a large, deep crimson disk approximately 1.8° in angular diameter — roughly 3.5 times the apparent diameter of Earth's full moon, and clearly resolvable as a disk by the naked eye. During periods when Aeterion is below the horizon and Umbrix remains above it, Prima experiences a dim red-tinged twilight-day: light levels are far below those of true daytime but sufficient for vision, and all surfaces are bathed in deep crimson light with soft, diffuse shadows. This condition has no precise analogue on Earth and has profoundly shaped the aesthetic, religious, and philosophical traditions of Prima's peoples.

Because Umbrix moves against the stellar background on a 139-year orbital cycle, its position shifts measurably within a human lifetime and dramatically across generations. This slow drift — too gradual for a single observer to fully witness but unmistakable when records are compared across centuries — has produced a class of *generational calendars* that use Umbrix's position as a long-period epoch marker.

Transits of Umbrix across the face of Aeterion are geometrically possible but extremely rare, owing to the 6° orbital inclination. When they occur, Umbrix's disk would be visible as a large dark-red circle moving slowly across Aeterion, blocking a significant portion of its light — a striking and historically significant event unlike anything in ordinary experience on Prima.

### Sekara

Sekara is the brightest object in Prima's night sky. Because of its L5 trojan configuration, it maintains a near-constant angular separation of approximately 60° from Aeterion, appearing as a permanent evening star in the west after sunset and disappearing below the horizon a few hours later. It is never visible at midnight from any latitude. Its dark, reddish surface and reported luminous anomalies have made it a culturally charged object.

### Other planets and bodies

Varun is the brightest morning and evening star after Sekara, owing to its reflective cloud cover. Keveron is visible as a bright, slowly moving point. Drakis and Nycthor require sustained observation to track but are detectable with the naked eye under dark skies. Vandraren appears once every several generations as a brilliant comet traversing the sky at a steep angle, always arriving from an unexpected direction due to its polar orbit. Umbrix, though far dimmer than Aeterion, is unmistakably present as a large crimson disk throughout the night sky whenever it is above the horizon.

## Orbital dynamics and stability

The critical stability radius for planets orbiting Aeteras A — the maximum semi-major axis at which a prograde circular orbit can remain stable against perturbation from Umbrix — is approximately 9.6 AU, based on the separation and mass ratio of the two stars. All eight known planets lie well within this limit, with Nycthor at 8.1 AU being the closest to the boundary. Nycthor's orbit is expected to show century-scale perturbations that could, in principle, be detected by long-term astronomical programmes on Prima.

The barycentre of the binary system lies approximately 8.6 AU from Aeteras A — between the orbits of Keveron (3.2 AU) and Nycthor (8.1 AU). Both stars orbit this common centre of mass on their 139-year cycle.

Vandraren's orbit crosses the gravitational domains of both stellar components on each revolution, making its long-term trajectory subject to chaotic perturbation over sufficiently long timescales. Its current orbital parameters are well-defined within observational records but may not be stable over millions of years.
