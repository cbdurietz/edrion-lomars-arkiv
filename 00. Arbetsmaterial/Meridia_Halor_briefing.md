# Briefing: Sötvatten, halorsten och Huset Halor

*A worldbuilding briefing for Meridia. This document captures the concepts, logic and decisions reached in development. It is NOT finished article prose — it is source material to hand to the writing model alongside the existing corpus, so the actual `Meridia`-register texts stay tonally coherent. In-world terms are kept in Swedish; explanatory prose is in English. All of this is provisional and editable.*

---

## 0. How to use this document

- This briefing supplies **facts, structure and dramatic logic**. Tone, register and phrasing should come from the existing Swedish texts (the `Meridia` and `Meridias familjer` articles and the wider corpus), not from this document.
- Where this briefing touches existing canon, it tries to *extend* rather than contradict. A few points need reconciliation with texts not seen during development (notably `Meridias kvarterer` and `Meridias historia`) — these are flagged as **OPEN**.
- Nothing here is locked. Several deliberate decisions are recorded with their *reasoning*, so the writer understands why a choice was made and can overrule it knowingly.

---

## 1. Core premise: desalination is the missing third act of an existing story

Meridia's canon already establishes freshwater as the city's deepest vulnerability:

- The island has a natural spring and rainwater cisterns/aqueducts, but these only ever sufficed for a small settlement — not the city Meridia became.
- For a couple of hundred `cyklader`, freshwater has been the city's most tangible weakness: it cannot feed itself, and any prolonged drought or blockade is a threat the walls cannot stop.
- The cistern-and-import system is described as an engineering masterpiece that is also *"ett erkännande av ett misslyckande man aldrig direkt kommer att erkänna."*
- Imported water arrives via **Vattenkajen** at **Norrehamn**.

Desalination is therefore not a new feature bolted on — it is the resolution the existing material is reaching toward. The historical arc of Meridia's water supply has **four phases**:

1. **Natural spring + rainwater cisterns** — sustains the original settlement, insufficient for the city.
2. **Imported water (Vattenkajen)** — the strategic lifeline, and a quiet humiliation: the city that defines where the world begins cannot fill its own cups.
3. **Coral desalination (the Halor fields)** — recent. Turns the sea itself into the supply. Changes the existential calculus, because **seawater cannot be blockaded.**
4. **(Ongoing) the demotion of imported water** — see §6.

**Key reframing to preserve:** desalination does not *erase* the vulnerability, it *relocates* it. The blockade/drought threat fades; the new soft target is the fields themselves — exposed on the shallow southwestern flats, biological, slow, foulable. This relocation is the seed of the family's central conflict (§4).

**Thematic payoff:** the city's self-image is that it *measures and transforms the world to its own benefit* (`tar emot allt och sorterar om det till sin egen nytta`). Desalination is that instinct made literal — Meridia now drinks the sea and builds its terraces out of what it strains from it. The fields are the city's character rendered physical.

## 2. How the desalination works (the fields)

- A cultivated **salt-sequestering coral** grown in managed **tidal basins** on the shallow, warm flats of **Gryningshavet** (the western, shallow, coastal sea — consistent with canon, which describes Gryningshavet as grunt/kustnära and Skymningshavet as djupare/stormigare).
- Mechanism (kept deliberately simple, "magic-adjacent natural philosophy" rather than hard magic): the coral builds its skeleton by locking dissolved salt into its growing structure; the water left in the basin slowly freshens and is drawn off. Flood at high tide, let the coral work, draw off the freshened water, repeat.
- **Growth is slow and seasonal.** This is the built-in scarcity: production barely outpaces demand, so the city retains a permanent low-grade water anxiety even with the fields running. (Preserve the canonical "thin buffer" — historically framed as only a couple of days' reserve.)
- The coral is **fussy**: it needs clean water, the right temperature and flow. This is what makes the reload harbours a threat (§5, Orren).
- When the coral is spent (too salt-dense and brittle to grow further) it is harvested and broken down — the input to halorsten (§3).

**OPEN — geography to reconcile with `Meridias kvarterer`:** exactly where the fields sit relative to Norrehamn, the second harbour, and Vattenkajen. The working assumption is: fields on the shallow Gryningshavet side; the dirty reload harbours and the coral fields are in tension over the same coastal water (see §5). The direction of the **strait current** around the island is a live variable: if it carries harbour filth toward the fields, the Orren rivalry is *hot and literal*; if it carries filth away, the rivalry is about money and coast-rights rather than survival. **Decide this deliberately.**

## 3. Halorsten — the material

**What it is:** not a quarried stone but a **formulated composite**, terrazzo-like. Spent, salt-saturated coral (originally a waste/landfill problem) is broken down, bound into a matrix, and ground/polished. It is manufactured, not mined — unmistakably modern, which matters socially (you cannot pretend halorsten is ancient).

**Origin as part of the family arc:** the coral waste was first merely *dumped as fill* (2nd generation), and only *later* developed into a graded composite product (3rd generation). See §4.

**Grades — a single waste stream, opposite ends of the market:**
- **Low grade** — binder-heavy, fine waste chips, minimal finish. Reads as a cheap stone substitute. Used in harbour-district walls, warehouse floors. Ubiquitous, structural, beneath notice. This is the lowercase `halorsten`.
- **High grade** — selects for large, intact, heavily salt-saturated coral pieces (the most crystalline), set in a tighter matrix and polished to bring out a glimmering shine where light catches the strained salt in the coral lattice. This is **`halorisk sten`**, the export/luxury designation — what you face a council chamber or a wealthy terrace with.
- The Halors control the **grading**, which is where both margin and prestige live. They profit from the warehouse floor and the senatorial atrium at once, with every incentive to keep the high grade rare and named while the low grade floods everywhere.

**The "cheap vs. beautiful" disagreement is a feature, not a question with an answer.** "Some think it a low-cost replacement for real stone; others praise the shine" — this is a *class marker the same family sells both sides of*. Old houses can use it as a double bind: is your gleaming halorisk feature wall a sign you've arrived, or a confession you furnished with *strained brine* because you couldn't afford marble?

**Hygroscopic tell (texture + plausibility):** salt-bound, so halorsten can weep/bloom salt and sweat in damp weather. Make this a **grade problem** — cheap binder-heavy halorsten betrays itself over time in the harbour damp (needs sealing, blooms white); high grade, tighter and better polished, resists it. Gives masons opinions and buyers something to be cheated on.

**The lowercase sting (usable plot/relationship beat):** in Swedish, a material named after a proper noun drifts to lowercase once genericized (cf. *champagne*, *manchester*). The day people stop writing *Halorsten* and write *halorsten*, the family name has become a common noun — sold by the block, cheap by definition. Cuts both ways: triumph (their name is in the very fabric of the city) or a wound the old houses press on (*your name is literally building material now*). Merí, who spends symbolic capital `med kirurgisk precision`, would know exactly which reading to imply to which audience.

**Name / etymology:** "hal-" functions as the world's **salt root** (cf. Greek *hals*). So `halorsten` reads, to anyone who traces it, as *salt-stone* — literally what it is. Reuse the existing Merí motif of strategic ambiguity: just as it is deliberately left open whether Merí named the city or the city named Merí, leave it open whether the family took its name from the stone or the stone from the family. A new house borrowing the old houses' favourite trick is both very Meridian and a little presumptuous — which suits new money. Adjective: **`halorsk`** (→ `halorsk sten`).

## 4. Huset Halor — the family

> Heading form, pending confirmation: **`Huset Halor`** (matching `Huset Merí`, `Huset Vaen`). Family adjective `halorsk`; member-plural convention (e.g. `halorerna` vs `Halor-familjen`) **OPEN — to match the writer's established habit.**

**`Maktbas`:** Sötvattenproduktion (avsaltning via korallfälten) och halorsten (kompositmaterial). Two pillars that mean different things:
- **Water = legitimacy / belonging.** The founding moral claim. The retort to every old-house sneer: Merí drew the first rope across the strait, but a Halor gave the city something to *drink*. By Meridian standards the Halors are arrivistes (3–4 generations is an afternoon), yet they hold the one card the centuries-old houses cannot trump — they keep the city *alive*, and they know it. Water is why they get to feel they belong.
- **Stone = wealth and fashionability.** Halorsten is how they got *rich* and *fashionable* — their name on feature walls. The glamour is, in the seniors' framing, a means; the water is the end (stone revenue subsidizes the fields, the cliff-pumps, the basins).

**`Rådsrelation`:** **No seat — but closer to one than anyone else** (closer than Kael). The interesting nuance: their warrant for a seat is *stronger* than Kael's (keeping the city alive beats selling navigation instruments). So the question isn't "can they buy in" but "why are they still *out* when their function is so plainly load-bearing." The answer: seating them means the old houses publicly conceding the water-claim — the exact concession Merí and Thalis least want to make, because it rewrites the city's story about who matters. The Halors are kept at the threshold not because they are insufficient but because admitting them is too consequential. They are *owed*, and being made to wait for what you're owed is its own humiliation (felt most by the 3rd generation; see below).

**`Karaktär`:** Indispensable like Orren — but, unlike Orren (who is `fullständigt ointresserade av vad folk tycker om dem`), the Halors **want the respect, badly.** The founders' moral claim has curdled, by the 3rd generation, into status anxiety, and the stone is where that caring shows. New money that holds the strongest possible card and can't quite get the old houses to bow.

---

### The four-generation arc (the heart of the family)

Each generation solved the previous one's problem and created the next one's. By Meridian standards this is barely any time at all — which is itself part of the family's predicament.

- **1st generation — made the water.** The existential act; the founding moral standing. **Crucially, they knew the fields could fail** — they built the first basins and watched early ones die. This concrete knowledge of *how the system fails* is the family's most important asset and is being lost.
- **2nd generation — managed the consequence.** Dealt with the mounting spoil/waste from successful production; offered it as landfill/fill. Unglamorous, custodial. Also (subtly) protecting the *water's good name*: you don't let the thing that keeps the city alive become "the family that buried the western flats in rubble."
- **3rd generation — the current seniors. Made the family fashionable.** Invented halorsten and the grading system; turned waste into a graded, named, sold product (cheap structural + glittering `halorisk sten`). They reach for legitimacy and a seat; they want to be seen as a *house*, not a successful workshop. **They are stone-and-grade people, merchants and craftsmen — NOT natural-philosophers of the coral.** They know slowness works without fully knowing *why*; they inherited the practice, not the founding science.
- **4th generation — rising, the current tension.** Want to **rationalize the fields and increase turnaround** of the desalination itself. They invoke the water too: a growing city needs *volume*, a thicker buffer. (See §7 for the Cassel-culture source of their thinking.)

---

### The central tension (state this carefully — it is the engine)

**Both sides argue in the name of the water, and both genuinely mean it.**

- **3rd gen (seniors):** the fields are slow because slow is what keeps them alive *and* keeps the high grade glittering. You children want to push a living system you've never seen sicken.
- **4th gen (heirs):** the city doesn't care about your luxury chip-size; it needs throughput. Your reverence for slowness is just protecting your margin dressed up as piety.

Each can accuse the other of using the city's survival as cover for a business preference — **and each would be partly right.** The seniors really are protecting the high grade; the heirs really are chasing returns. But neither is lying about caring whether Meridia drinks. *No one is the cynic — they are all believers*, which is exactly how the thing they worship gets broken.

**Why speed specifically threatens the seniors' business:** the high grade (`halorisk sten`) depends on large, intact, slowly-grown, heavily salt-saturated coral. Push the fields, harvest younger and faster → smaller, less crystalline, less infused chips → *more low grade, less high grade*. The 4th generation, treating the fields as a water-and-volume problem, would be optimizing for the very thing that destroys the prestige product. The 3rd generation may not even articulate this cleanly — they just *feel* the children are about to kill the golden goose, and the children hear sentimentality.

**The 3rd generation's trap:** protecting the slow fields, protecting the water, and protecting the high grade all point the same direction — so they genuinely cannot tell their reverence from their self-interest. That is more human and more damning than a simple goose-and-golden-egg calculation.

**The buried existential danger (dramatic irony to leave mostly unspoken):** rushing the fields risks *fouling them and threatening the city's actual water supply*. The 1st generation understood this from experience. By the 4th generation it is myth, not memory. So the family venerates the water in the abstract, argues constantly in its name, and **has lost the concrete knowledge of how it actually fails.** They could push the fields over the edge while sincerely believing they are its truest defenders. The truth is on the seniors' side; the *language* is on the juniors' — and language usually wins a dinner table.

**Optional device:** a surviving 1st-generation notebook, an old retainer, or a Cassel ledger-note from the founding loans that "remembers what the family forgot" about how a field dies.

## 5. Relationships (`Spänningar`) — mapped onto existing houses

- **Orren (omlastning/hamninfrastruktur):** the core operational rivalry. The reload harbours' dirty water (bilge, sewage, spill) is exactly what the fussy coral cannot tolerate. Fight over bilge rules, basin siting, rights to the coast. Possible second collision over construction (Orren's skeppsvarv/building interests vs the growing halorsten terraces and materials trade). **Transactional, not blood feud** — Orren `arbetar gärna med vem som helst om siffrorna stämmer`. Intensity depends on the strait-current decision (§2).
- **Vaen (flottan/passagekontroll):** water-security overlap. Vaen "controls the water" (the passage) and their `lotsar` already know the Gryningshavet shallows. **Decision: Vaen does NOT own desalination** — giving them both the navy and the water would stack two existential chokeholds in one house and would waste the "new wealth" theme that desalination is the right vehicle for. Vaen is a rival/peer to negotiate with, not the owner.
- **Oshari (östhandel/utländska kontakter):** **gets the demoted luxury imported-water trade** (§6). Foreign luxury goods on eastern routes is already their lane; nice sting in the *outsider* family controlling the water the old houses now pay a premium for.
- **Cassel (finans/sjöförsäkring):** see §7.
- **Merí & Thalis (gammal legitimitet):** resent the Halors as the newest of new money (beneath even Kael), but **cannot fully dismiss them** because of the water-claim — so the posture is *resentment*, not contempt. They keep Halor at the threshold (§4). Merí holds the lowercase-`halorsten` jab in reserve.
- **Kael (navigationsinstrument):** structural kin — both rising applied-ingenuity houses on the `gammal legitimitet vs. ny rikedom` axis, both new money the old houses look down on. Room for either solidarity (fellow climbers) or rivalry (competing for the same "next seat"). Note Kael is also a Cassel-adjacent climber, which rhymes with §7.

## 6. The demoted Water Port (Vattenkajen)

Imported water's strategic rationale has **faded into luxury**. Once the lifeline, now a status good:
- **Imported mainland spring water** = a luxury the wealthy drink *because it did not come from the sea* (and is arguably higher quality / cleaner-tasting than the desalinated blend).
- **Desalinated blend** = what everyone else drinks — faintly mineral, faintly saline.
- This **rhymes with the halorsten grade split** (luxury `halorisk sten` vs common `halorsten`): the same society sorting itself by provenance, the rich defined by *not* using the sea-sourced version of the thing.
- Trade in the luxury water → **Oshari** (§5).
- Faintly absurd, therefore very Meridian: the city that defines where the world begins now treats spring water as a delicacy.

## 7. Cassel and the "management" hubris (the cruellest beat)

**Debt history:** Cassel financed much of the Halors' initial investment (basins, cliff-pumps, the standing-up of the industry — staggering capital). **The Halors have since repaid most of it** — a rare near-escape from Cassel's book (`knappast en person av betydelse i Meridia som inte är skyldig Cassel någonting`). This makes the Halors quietly proud (we paid our way to freedom) and makes Cassel quietly attentive (a debtor who escapes is a relationship to re-establish on new terms).

**The 4th generation's flaw has a *source*:** the young Halors are close to the **young Cassels**, and have absorbed a growing **finance/"business management" culture** — rationalization, revenue streams, idle-asset thinking, throughput — as the drivers, *in place of* actual knowledge of the industry. This is, deliberately, the psychology of the modern **management consultant**: fluent, confident, generalizable, and empty of the one thing that matters here (the irreplaceable particular knowledge of the coral). Rendered in a pre-modern guild world there is no "MBA" to name it — it is just a way of talking the bright young heirs have caught from each other like a fashion. **Their flaw is epistemological: they have mistaken a way of talking about *any* business for understanding of *this* one.**

**The beat:** the Halors spent three generations climbing *out* from under Cassel. Now the heirs — who never felt the founding debt's weight, because their parents lifted it — will, of their own accord, propose **re-leveraging the house to accelerate the fields**, sincerely, as their own bright idea. Both the impulse to mortgage again *and* the framework that makes it sound smart arrive through the same social channel. **Cassel does not have to scheme.** Per their documented power — `inte att befalla utan att avvakta` — Cassel only has to wait; the young Halors walk back through the door cheerfully because their friends are inside. The family's hard-won independence is spent not by a trap but by the heirs returning on their own. The 3rd generation can see it happening and cannot name it without sounding like paranoid old people slandering their children's perfectly nice friends.

## 8. Placement on `Strukturella axlar` and `Rådet i praktiken`

- Halor sits firmly on **`Gammal legitimitet vs. ny rikedom`** alongside Kael and Orren — and *sharpens* that axis, because halorsten is so visibly manufactured/modern.
- Halor **introduces/intensifies a water-and-survival dimension** the table doesn't yet name (the founders' existential claim vs. everyone else's symbol/knowledge/coin).
- Note the unusual feature: **the `legitimitet vs. rikedom` axis also runs *inside* the family** — 3rd generation reaching for old-house respectability vs. 4th generation frankly industrial and indifferent to it.
- `Rådet i praktiken` note: Kael `knackar på`; Halor knocks *harder and with a better claim*, and is the house most likely to next force the question of a seat — possibly driven by the 3rd generation (who crave the legitimacy), while the 4th generation may regard the seat as a vanity distracting from margin. That internal disagreement about *whether the seat even matters* is itself usable.

## 10. One-paragraph summary (if the writer wants the gist fast)

Huset Halor are recent, rich, and not-quite-respectable — a four-generation house whose founders learned to draw fresh water from the shallow Gryningshavet by farming a salt-sequestering coral, and whose later generations turned the spent coral into `halorsten`, a graded terrazzo-like composite that now floors half the harbour and gleams on the walls of the wealthy as `halorisk sten`. Water is their moral claim (they keep the city alive, which even Merí cannot trump); stone is their fortune and their faint vulgarity. They have nearly escaped Cassel's ledger and stand closer to a council seat than any unseated house — kept out only because seating them would force the old houses to admit how load-bearing they are. The present crisis is internal: a 3rd generation of stone-and-grade craftsmen who revere the slow fields without quite knowing why, against a 4th generation steeped in the young Cassels' revenue-stream thinking who want to rationalize and accelerate production — both sides arguing in the name of the water, neither remembering, as the founders did, that a pushed field can sicken and that the city is always only a little way from thirst.
